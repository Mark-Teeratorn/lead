## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): This dimension asks whether the way the study was designed, the way its data were handled, the way its statistics are reported and the means it gives others to repeat the work reach the standard a peer reviewer in the field would expect. From my seat as a safety-assurance and scenario-based validation reviewer, this is the ground that any reader from an adjacent field has to stand on before trusting a measure, but it is owned by the methodology seat and I am not eligible to score it.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This dimension asks whether what the manuscript asserts matches the current state of evidence in its own field, whether earlier work is described correctly, and whether field-specific terms and results are free of factual error. For a reader coming from outside, wrong or loose use of field terms is what most often leads to a wrong reading in a neighbouring discipline, but the judgement belongs to the domain seat and I do not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence shown actually carries the claims made from it, and whether the main argument is free of reasoning errors that would undo it. I rely on this when I ask what an outside reader may safely take away, but checking logic and internal consistency is the work of the devil's-advocate and methodology seats, not mine, and I do not score it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This dimension asks whether the framing, the definitions and the stated implications can be followed and used correctly by readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches into another discipline is backed by evidence that discipline would accept. This is my dimension. From a safety-assurance and validation standpoint I read it as: are the measures and terms defined well enough that an outside reader cannot mistake them for something stronger, are any claims that touch safety, deployment or real-world use supported to the standard of the field they touch, and are stated limits on interpretation kept consistently from the title to the conclusions.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension asks whether the manuscript is well organised, clearly written, supported by figures and tables of adequate quality, and in line with the conventions of where it is to be published. Clear structure decides how much an outside reader can follow, but the editor-in-chief seat owns this and I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether the manuscript suits its configured venue and offers a contribution that is new and significant for that venue's readers. This call is not bound to a venue, I make no claim about venue fit, and the dimension belongs to the editor-in-chief seat, so I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether every measure and key term is defined operationally so that a safety-assurance or validation reader can tell what was actually measured and what was not; whether any claim of a trade-off rests on a valid and reliable measure on each axis and on enough operating points to describe a relation; whether wording in the title, abstract, discussion and conclusions stays within the scope the manuscript itself declares, including any stated limit on safety or deployment interpretation; whether implications for practitioners outside the home field are stated and are backed by evidence that the adjacent field would accept; and whether the described materials would let an outside group repeat the work.
what_triggers_block: A claim that reaches into an adjacent field, such as a safety, risk, deployment or real-world implication, is stated as a finding without evidence that the adjacent field would accept, or a central term or measure is left undefined or is used in a sense that an adjacent-field reader would reasonably take as a stronger property than the evidence supports, so that the main message would be misread outside the home field.
what_triggers_warn: The framing and definitions can be followed by an adjacent-field reader with effort, but one or more of the following holds in a way that does not change the main message: a key term or measure is defined late or loosely, a stated limit on interpretation is not kept consistently in every part of the manuscript, a trade-off or implication is worded more broadly than the measures and operating points allow, or the practical implications for readers outside the home field are missing or only gestured at.
