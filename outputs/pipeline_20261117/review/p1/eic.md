## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): From the editorial chair this dimension asks whether the way a study was designed, the way its data were handled, the way its statistics are reported and the means it gives others to repeat the work reach the standard that peer review in the field expects. It is mandatory and belongs to the methodology seat; I do not score it, and I read that seat's verdict as an input to the overall picture and not as something I re-derive.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This dimension asks whether what a submission asserts agrees with the current state of evidence in its field, whether earlier work is described correctly, and whether domain terms and results are used without factual error. It is mandatory and owned by the domain seat; I do not score it, and my editorial interest is limited to whether a domain error, if that seat finds one, would change what the work can be said to contribute.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence offered carries the claims placed on it, and whether the reasoning is free of fallacies that would undo the main argument. It is mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate seat with the methodology seat also eligible; I do not score it, although the match between headline statements and supporting evidence is something I also watch from the structural side under my own dimensions.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This dimension asks whether framing, definitions and implications can be followed by readers from neighbouring fields and whether any claims that reach across disciplines are backed up. It is high priority and owned by the perspective seat; I do not score it, and I treat it as the panel's check on how far beyond the home community the work can travel.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension asks whether a manuscript is organised sensibly, explained clearly, supported by figures and tables of adequate quality, and written to the conventions a scholarly outlet expects. It is normal priority and I own it; from the editor's seat it is the question of whether a reader can find the question, the method, the results and the limits in the places where they should be, at a length the content justifies, and whether the document is complete enough to be reviewed as submitted.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether a manuscript suits the outlet it is aimed at and offers a contribution that is original and significant for that outlet's readers. It is mandatory and I own it. No venue criteria are bound to this call, so I commit to judging the contribution half at a field-general level, meaning originality, significance and whether the title, abstract and conclusion promise only what the work delivers, and I will make no statement about alignment with any particular venue.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure

dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Consistency of the line from title to abstract to introduction to conclusion; a clearly stated research question that the conclusion answers; logical section order; length in proportion to content; division of material between main text and any supplement such that the main text can be read by itself; figures and tables that are legible, correctly numbered, cited in the text and consistent with the prose; completeness of the document as submitted, with no unfilled content; consistent terminology and general scholarly conventions.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript cannot be assessed as submitted because of its presentation, for example the main text depends on material that is missing or left unfilled, the organisation prevents a reader from locating the question, method, results or limits, or figures and tables contradict the text or cannot be read.
what_triggers_warn: The manuscript can be assessed but presentation problems cost the reader real effort, for example length out of proportion to content, repetition, a main text that leans on the supplement for essential steps, inconsistent terminology or numbering, or figures and tables that need rework to be clear.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: What the core contribution is and what its source of originality is (new data, method, framework, perspective or combination); whether it fills a gap or repeats what is already known; how far its significance reaches if the conclusions hold; whether the scope of the evidence is declared and matches the generality of the headline statements; whether title, abstract and conclusion promise only what is delivered; whether the main message is stated in a form that does not depend on results not yet reported; no venue-alignment judgement is made because no venue criteria are bound.
what_triggers_block: The contribution as presented is not yet sufficient or not yet established for publication but could become so through revision, for example the headline statements in title, abstract or conclusion go clearly beyond what the reported evidence supports, the novelty over prior work is not demonstrated, or the main message rests on results that are announced but not reported.
what_triggers_warn: A real contribution is present but is framed more broadly than its evidence base, positioned incompletely against prior work, or stated with significance that is narrower than claimed, such that rewording and tighter scoping would resolve the concern without new work.
what_triggers_fatal: There is no original contribution that revision could establish, because the central result is already known in the literature, or the central claim would not hold under any outcome of the work as designed, so that no rewriting or rescoping would leave a publishable message.
