## Contract Paraphrase

D1 methodology_rigor (mandatory; I am the only eligible seat and the owner). I read this as the question of whether the study, as designed and analysed, can answer the questions it sets itself to the standard a careful referee in this field would demand. For me that covers four things: the design (what was varied, what was held fixed, what was replicated and at which level), the handling of data (unit of analysis, pairing, exclusions, failed or repeated runs), the statistical reporting (pre-specified versus post hoc status of each test, effect sizes with intervals, treatment of multiplicity, and whether measured run-to-run noise is carried into every headline uncertainty), and the reproducibility affordances (code, configurations, logs and decision records sufficient for another group to rerun the work). Because the dimension is mandatory, I must separate in advance a defect that further analysis of existing evidence can repair from one that leaves no valid analysis at all.

D2 domain_accuracy (mandatory; domain seat only). This asks whether the manuscript's statements about its field are correct: whether prior work is represented faithfully, whether domain terms and benchmark conventions are used as the field uses them, and whether its claims sit correctly against current evidence. I do not score it. From a methodology standpoint my only contact with it is indirect: if a measurement or benchmark convention matters to the validity of an analysis, I judge the analysis under D1 and leave the question of domain correctness to the domain seat.

D3 argumentative_coherence (mandatory; devil's advocate seat owns it, and I am also eligible). This asks whether the central thesis holds together and whether the evidence presented actually carries the claims made from it. I approach it as a question of inferential licence: does each claim go no further than the design and the tests allow, is causal or attributional language matched by a design that isolates the cause, are confirmatory and exploratory results kept apart in the argument, are mixed or unreplicated results described as such, and do the abstract and conclusions say the same thing as the results and the stated limitations. I plan a score here, limited to the fit between claims and evidence rather than rhetorical quality.

D4 cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority; perspective seat only). This asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field can follow the framing and definitions, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplines is backed by something more than assertion. I do not score it. Where borrowed statistical or pre-specification vocabulary is used, whether it is used correctly is a D1 matter for me; whether it is accessible and relevant to adjacent readers belongs to the perspective seat.

D5 writing_and_structure (normal priority; editor seat only). This asks whether the manuscript is organised sensibly, written clearly, and supported by readable figures and tables in the form its outlet expects. I do not score it. A table or figure that omits information needed to audit an analysis is a reporting gap I treat under D1; presentation quality as such is not mine to judge.

D6 venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory; editor seat only). This asks whether the work belongs in the outlet it is aimed at and offers its readers something original and significant. I do not score it and I form no view on it. This call carries no criteria binding, so I make no statement about fit to any venue.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Whether the design can answer the stated questions; whether every confirmatory rule (hypothesis, outcome, test, threshold, exclusion rule, stopping rule) was fixed before the data it governs existed, with a dated record and disclosure of any interim looks; whether the unit of analysis matches the unit of inference and pairing is respected; whether the tests suit the sample size and outcome distribution; whether run-to-run noise is measured and carried into the uncertainty of each headline contrast; whether each test reports an effect size with an interval; whether the family of tests is delimited, multiplicity is handled, and post hoc tests are labelled as such; whether training-side variance and factors that vary together are accounted for; whether exclusions and failed runs are fully accounted for; and whether code, configurations, logs and decision records permit an independent rerun.
what_triggers_block: A headline result rests on an analysis that is mis-specified yet repairable from evidence already collected or cheaply collectable, such as a test that ignores the pairing or the replication structure, an uncertainty statement that omits measured run-to-run noise, a confirmatory conclusion drawn from a family of unadjusted tests, a result labelled pre-specified whose rule cannot be shown to predate its data, or missing materials without which the main analysis cannot be rerun.
what_triggers_warn: The main analyses are sound but the reporting has gaps that do not change the conclusions, such as effect sizes or intervals missing for secondary tests, exploratory tests not consistently labelled, incomplete accounting of exclusions or reruns, pre-specification wording that is looser than the record supports, or partial reproducibility materials.
what_triggers_fatal: The evidential basis of the core finding is unrecoverable by any re-analysis, because the primary outcome or its decision rule was set or changed after the governing data were seen while being presented as fixed in advance and no rule fixed beforehand supports the finding, or the evaluation is not independent of the data used to fit or select the compared systems, or the reported numbers cannot be reconciled with the described procedure.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether each claim in the abstract, results and conclusions goes no further than the design and tests allow; whether attribution to a named cause is matched by a design that isolates that cause; whether confirmatory and exploratory findings are kept apart in the argument; whether results that hold in only some replications are described as mixed; whether each side of any claimed trade-off is supported by a difference distinguishable from noise; and whether the stated limitations agree with the strength of the headline wording.
what_triggers_block: A central claim is stated more strongly than the design permits and must be re-scoped or re-argued, such as an effect attributed to one factor when several vary together, one side of a claimed trade-off resting on a difference not distinguishable from run-to-run noise, a hypothesis met in only part of the replications presented as confirmed, or a post hoc result carrying confirmatory weight in the argument.
what_triggers_warn: The core thesis is supported, but wording in places outruns the evidence and can be corrected by editing, such as causal phrasing for an associational result, a limitation acknowledged in one section and ignored in another, or secondary claims that lean on exploratory analyses without saying so.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is contradicted by the manuscript's own reported results, or depends wholly on an inference the design cannot support in any narrower form, so that no rewording and no re-analysis of the existing evidence leaves a defensible core claim.
