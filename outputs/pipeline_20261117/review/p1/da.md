## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): This dimension asks whether the way the study was designed, the way its data were handled, the way its statistics are reported and the means it gives others to reproduce it reach the standard a peer reviewer in the field would demand. It is mandatory and belongs to the methodology seat, so I will not score it. From the adversarial side I read it as the question of whether each number the argument leans on could have come out the same way for a reason other than the one the authors give, and I remain free to report any such weakness as a finding even though the score is not mine.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This dimension asks whether the claims agree with what the field currently knows, whether earlier work is described as it actually is, and whether field-specific terms and results are stated without factual error. It is mandatory and belongs to the domain seat, so I will not score it. As the challenger I treat it as a check on whether the surrounding literature has been described in a way that makes the contribution look larger or the opposing evidence look smaller than it is; I may raise such a point as a finding without scoring it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together with itself, whether the evidence presented actually carries the claims made from it, and whether the main argument is free of fallacies that would bring it down. It is mandatory, I own it, and the methodology seat may also score it. This is where my challenge concentrates: I will build the strongest case against the main conclusion, look for a simpler rival account that fits the same evidence, trace each inferential step for hidden premises, and test whether the stated scope of the conclusions stays inside what the evidence covers.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This dimension asks whether the framing, the definitions and the stated implications can be followed by readers from neighbouring fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplines is backed up. It is high priority and belongs to the perspective seat, so I will not score it. My adversarial reading is narrower: a claim that borrows authority from another field without support is an unsupported premise, and I may report it as a finding where it bears on the argument.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension asks whether the manuscript is well organised, clearly explained, served by its figures and tables, and in line with the conventions of its venue. It is normal priority and belongs to the editor seat, so I will not score it. I attend to it only where unclear exposition hides a gap in reasoning or makes a claim impossible to evaluate, in which case the underlying gap is what I report.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether the manuscript suits the configured venue and offers an original and significant contribution for that readership. It is mandatory and belongs to the editor seat, so I will not score it, and because this call carries no criteria binding I make no statement about venue alignment. The only part I touch as challenger is the bare question of what would remain of value if the central argument failed, raised as a finding rather than a score.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the central thesis is stated the same way wherever it appears; whether each conclusion follows from the evidence offered for it; whether causal or mechanistic language is matched by evidence able to separate the proposed account from simpler rivals; whether premises are stated or hidden; whether evidence that cuts against the thesis is reported and weighed on the same terms as evidence for it; whether the scope of the conclusions stays within the conditions actually examined; and whether stated limitations are carried through to the wording of the conclusions.
what_triggers_block: A core claim does not follow from the evidence presented for it, or rests on an unsupported premise, or leaves unaddressed a rival explanation that fits the presented evidence at least as well, such that the claim cannot stand as written but could be repaired by further analysis, narrowed scope, or rewording.
what_triggers_warn: A secondary claim or a link in the reasoning is overstated, generalised beyond the conditions examined, or supported by selectively weighed evidence, while the central thesis still stands on the evidence presented.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is contradicted by the manuscript's own reported evidence, or is internally self-contradictory, or depends on reasoning that is circular or unfalsifiable, such that no revision of the present study could rescue the main conclusion.
