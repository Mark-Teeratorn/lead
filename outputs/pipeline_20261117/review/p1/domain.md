## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): This dimension asks whether the way a study was designed, how its data were collected and processed, how its statistics are reported, and what it offers for reproduction reach the standard a peer reviewer in the field would require. From the domain seat I do not score it; it belongs to the methodology reviewer. I only note that domain accuracy depends on it at one boundary: a domain claim can be worded correctly and still rest on a design that cannot carry it, and when I see that boundary I will describe the domain side and leave the design verdict to the owner.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This is my dimension and it is mandatory. It asks three things in the terms of the stated field, end-to-end driving and imitation learning for vision-language-action policies. First, whether each substantive claim agrees with what the field currently knows and with the evidence the manuscript itself gives for it. Second, whether earlier work is described as that work actually reports, attributed to the right source, and placed fairly in relation to the submission, including work that would compete with or qualify the contribution. Third, whether field terms, benchmark and metric names, model and method descriptions, and reported results are used in their accepted meaning without factual error. A domain reader should be able to rely on every such statement without checking it again.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence offered really supports each step, and whether any fallacy weakens the main argument. I do not score it; the devil's advocate owns it and the methodology reviewer may also score it. Seen from domain accuracy, the link is that an argument can be internally consistent yet built on a premise the field would not accept, so I will report a faulty domain premise under D2 and not pass judgement on the logic of the chain.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This dimension asks whether framing, definitions and implications can be followed by readers in neighbouring fields, and whether claims that reach into other disciplines are backed up. It is a high-priority dimension owned by the perspective reviewer and I do not score it. The domain view contributes only this: a term may be defined clearly for outsiders and still depart from how specialists use it, and that departure is a D2 matter, not a D4 one.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality of figures and tables, and conformity with venue conventions. It has normal priority, is owned by the editor-in-chief seat, and I do not score it. For domain accuracy the relevant edge is that unclear wording can hide whether a domain statement is right or wrong; where I cannot tell what is being claimed I will say so as a domain uncertainty and not as a writing verdict.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether a manuscript suits the configured venue and makes an original and significant contribution for that readership. It is mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief seat, and I do not score it. This call is not bound to a venue, so I make no statement about fit. The domain seat touches it only in that originality depends on prior work being represented correctly, which I assess under D2 as a question of accuracy and not as a judgement of significance.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each domain claim agrees with current evidence in end-to-end driving and imitation learning and with the evidence the manuscript itself supplies; whether cited prior work is attributed correctly and described as it actually reports; whether the closest prior and competing lines of work are acknowledged and the submission's difference from them is stated accurately; whether field terms, benchmark names, metric definitions, model and planner descriptions and reported results are used in their accepted meaning; whether any interpretation of a measured quantity is one that quantity can support in domain terms; whether stated novelty and scope match what the field already knows.
what_triggers_block: A domain claim central to the contribution is stated more strongly than the domain evidence allows and the wording must be rewritten or further domain evidence added before a specialist could rely on it; or the closest prior or competing work is omitted or described incorrectly in a way that changes how the contribution is positioned; or a field term, benchmark, metric or method is used in a sense that departs from accepted usage and the departure affects a main conclusion; or a measured quantity is given a domain interpretation that it cannot support without additional evidence; each of these being repairable by revision while the core finding could still stand.
what_triggers_warn: A domain statement that is not central to the contribution is imprecise, dated or loosely attributed; or a relevant but not closest line of prior work is missing or summarised too briefly; or a term or metric is used inconsistently without changing any conclusion; or a reference carries an error in authorship, year, venue or reported content that does not alter the argument; or a scope or generality statement is slightly wider than the domain evidence shown.
what_triggers_fatal: The core domain claim contradicts established domain evidence or the manuscript's own reported results and cannot be made correct by rewording or by adding analysis; or the contribution as stated already exists in prior work that is absent or misrepresented so that no accurate restatement leaves an original domain finding; or a factual error about the system, benchmark or metric under study invalidates the reported results themselves.
