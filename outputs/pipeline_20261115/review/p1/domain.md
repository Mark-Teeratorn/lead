## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): This dimension asks whether the way a study is designed, the way its data are handled, the way its statistics are reported and the means it offers for others to reproduce it reach the standard that peer review in the field expects. From the standpoint of domain accuracy I treat it as the neighbouring seat's responsibility: I do not score it, but I note that a domain reading depends on it, because a field-level claim can only be as sound as the evaluation protocol behind it. Where a domain concern turns out to be a design or inference concern, I hand it to the methodology seat instead of scoring it myself.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This is the dimension I own. It asks three things: that what a manuscript asserts agrees with what the field currently knows; that earlier work is described as it actually is, with the right findings attributed to the right sources; and that the technical vocabulary and the reported results of the field are used without factual error. In the field named in the metadata that means checking any statement about imitation-learned driving policies, their known failure modes, the benchmarks and metrics used to evaluate them, and the planner and controller components they rely on against the established literature, and checking that a manuscript places itself correctly among the lines of work it builds on or departs from.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence offered really carries the claims made, and whether any reasoning error weakens the main argument. It belongs to the devil's-advocate and methodology seats and I do not score it. Its link to domain accuracy is that an argument can be internally tidy and still rest on a premise the field has already qualified or refuted; I will report such a premise under D2 and leave the judgement on the argument's logic to the eligible seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This dimension asks whether framing, definitions and implications can be followed by readers from adjacent fields and whether any claim reaching beyond the home field is backed up. It belongs to the perspective seat and I do not score it. From the domain side the relevant point is narrower: when a term carries a specific meaning inside the field, an accessible restatement must not change that meaning, and I will flag such a shift as a terminology matter under D2.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension covers how a manuscript is organised, how clearly it is written, the quality of its figures and tables, and whether it follows the conventions of its outlet. It belongs to the editor-in-chief seat and I do not score it. I touch it only where presentation makes a domain statement ambiguous enough that its accuracy cannot be judged, and then I report the ambiguity as a domain issue, not as a writing issue.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether a manuscript suits its configured outlet and offers an original and significant contribution to that outlet's readers. It belongs to the editor-in-chief seat and I do not score it. This call is not bound to a venue, so I make no statement about fit. What I can supply from the domain side is input on novelty relative to prior work, that is, whether a claimed contribution is already present in the literature, and I will record that under D2 as a matter of how prior work is represented.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each field-level claim agrees with current evidence in end-to-end driving and imitation learning; whether cited prior work is described and attributed correctly and the references exist as given; whether the lines of work closest to the stated contribution are covered and the manuscript is positioned against them; whether the baselines and comparisons the field would expect are present or their absence is justified; whether field terms, benchmark names, metric definitions and component descriptions are used correctly; and whether the interpretation placed on a measured quantity is one that quantity can support according to how the field understands the underlying system.
what_triggers_block: A headline claim depends on a domain premise that is wrong or unsupported, or on an interpretation of a measure that the measure cannot carry given how the underlying system works, and a correction would require new analysis or new experiments; or a closely related body of prior work that bears directly on the main claim is omitted or misrepresented so that the claimed contribution cannot be assessed; or a baseline the field regards as necessary for the main claim is missing without justification.
what_triggers_warn: Domain statements are correct in substance but imprecise or overstated in places; individual references are inaccurate, misattributed or incomplete without affecting the main claim; relevant prior work is cited but engaged with too thinly; an expected baseline or comparison is absent but its absence is acknowledged and limits only a secondary claim; or a field term is used loosely in a way that text revision alone can repair.
what_triggers_fatal: The central finding contradicts well-established domain evidence without acknowledging or addressing it, or reproduces a result already established in prior work while presenting it as new, or rests on fabricated or non-existent sources or on a factual error about the field so basic that no revision of the present study could make the main conclusion hold.
