## B. Facts about the protocol that the manuscript does not state

1. **Early termination.** `scenario_manager.py` L183–192 stops a route after 4,000 ticks (200 s) or after 1,200 consecutive ticks (60 s) with ego speed below 0.1 m/s. Both appear as `Failed - TickRuntime`. The standstill fraction is therefore censored. Routes with that status among the 44 (ended by the 60 s rule / by the 4,000-tick cap): matched flooded 7 (7 / 0), Golden-6400 3 (3 / 0), historical flooded 4 (3 / 1), Golden-1600 5 (5 / 0), Flooded-1600 3 (1 / 2), Base 7 (0 / 7).
2. **The 60 s rule was introduced during data collection.** The file carrying it is dated 4 Oct 2026. By result-file dates, 38 of the 44 `Base` routes were evaluated before that date (29 Sep–3 Oct) and 6 after. `Base` was therefore measured mostly under a different termination rule from the four fine-tuned models: all seven of its `TickRuntime` routes ran to the 4,000-tick cap, where a fine-tuned model in the same situation would have been stopped after 60 s. This inflates `Base` standstill time relative to the other models and affects the `Base` rows of Tables 3–4 and the H1 comparison (Golden-1600 vs Base).
3. **Harness changes between the two arms of H2.** `leaderboard_evaluator.py`, `route_scenario.py` and the launcher were modified on 10 Nov 2026: large-map streaming settings applied only on Town12/13, synchronous mode and step set explicitly, a guard around `stop_scenario`, a guard for an empty parked-vehicle batch, and the client timeout raised from 60 s to 180 s. Golden-6400 was evaluated on 19–20 Oct, the matched flooded model on 11–12 Nov. The changes are expected to be behaviour-neutral on the canonical routes; this is tested by the replicate runs in `ANALYSIS_ADDENDUM.md` §4b.
4. **Collision counting.** The leaderboard merges repeated contacts with the same actor within 5 s or 5 m before logging. The manuscript's statement that every contact tick registers as an event is incorrect.
5. **Training schedules in Table 2 are wrong.** According to the A100 operational record (`CHAT_MATRIX_TRAINING_AND_RESUME.md`), all four Matrix models used effective batch 8 and 10 epochs: 2,000 optimizer steps at 1,600 frames and 8,000 at 6,400 frames. Table 2 lists 4,800 and 20,050 steps. The two 6,400-frame Matrix models therefore share a schedule; the historical flooded checkpoint (6,440 frames, 8 epochs, 6,415 steps) differs from them by about 20 % in steps.
6. **Controller configuration.** No controller threshold is configurable; the standstill gate keeps no internal state. What the manuscript calls a hysteretic latch is the clause `v < 0.5 m/s and L < 2.0 m` together with the holding brake.
7. **Controller tuning routes.** The PID and lookahead were tuned on canonical routes before the comparison (`docs/CONTROLLER_OPTIMIZATION_EXPERIMENT.md`); the routes and checkpoint used will be named and H2 repeated on the routes not used.

8. **Training and evaluation routes overlap.** The manuscript states that training routes are route-disjoint from `bench2drive220.xml`. The dataset records say otherwise: about 7.1–7.5 % of each training set's samples lie on routes that overlap one of the 220 evaluation routes, and about 1.0–1.4 % on routes overlapping the 44-route subset (lower bounds; see `docs/provenance/A100_TRAINING_PROVENANCE.md`). The overlap is similar in the Golden and Flooded sets, so it bears mainly on comparisons against `Base` (H1) and on the "unseen holdout" description of the 175 additional routes. Exact mapping with the gate file: training samples in the Matrix cells touch seven canonical routes (1852, 1825, 2286, 23901, 23695, 3464, 2164; 78 samples in golden_6400, 87 in flooded_6400), and four more canonical routes are gate-flagged in the pool. H2 without the seven: −7.23 pp, 95 % BCa CI [−15.31, +0.66], Wilcoxon p = 0.0065 (N = 37); without all eleven: −7.70 pp, CI [−16.59, +1.20], p = 0.016 (N = 33). H1 without the seven: +13.67 DS, CI [4.90, 23.11], p = 0.0065. (Added 12 Nov 2026 after the primary endpoint was computed.)
9. **"Hazard flooding" is a curated draw.** Golden and Flooded sets are both drawn from one verified pool of 34,132 samples by fitting different target marginals; they share 4,284 of 6,400 samples at the larger scale. The manuscript's description of the flooded regime as naive or uncurated oversampling is inaccurate. (Added 12 Nov 2026.)

## D. Run notes for the follow-up runs of 12–17 November 2026 (not part of the confirmatory record)

These runs are specified in `ANALYSIS_ADDENDUM.md` Sections 4–8. Nothing here changes the confirmatory H2 result. (Added 14 Nov 2026.)

1. **Two routes were relaunched after the simulator stopped without a score.** Route 26458 in `model_matrix_golden_6400_rep1` (13 Nov, 12:55) and route 28087 in `model_matrix_golden_6400_nogate` (13 Nov, 21:20) ended with exit code 134 and no score on the first attempt. Under the rerun rule of the queue (relaunch only when no score exists) each was started once more and completed. The partial outputs of the first attempts were moved to an `aborted_*` folder inside the route's output folder and are kept. No route was relaunched after it had a score. Source: `outputs/revision_suite_audit.log`.
2. **A launch bug in the second and third queue was fixed before either had started a job.** In `scripts/common/run_revision_suite2.sh` and `run_revision_suite3.sh` the environment prefix of the model-server command (offline flags and allocator settings) was attached to a variable assignment instead of the command. The scripts were corrected in commit `308b5406` and the second queue was restarted while it was still waiting; no route had been launched by the faulty version. The second pass of Golden-6400 reuses the model server started by the first queue, which had the correct environment.
3. **Decoupling arm added.** A fifth model, `Matrix-FloodedGroups-Balanced-6400`, was trained on the existing decoupling dataset on the same schedule as the two 6,400-sample Matrix models (8,000 steps, seed 42), after the primary result was known. Its analysis was fixed before training (`ANALYSIS_ADDENDUM.md` Section 6). The checkpoint is public on the Hugging Face Hub (`earth37815/Alpamayo-1.5-10B-Matrix-FloodedGroups-Balanced-6400-A15`, upload commit `e7e621f5`); the checksum of the export archive and of its seven files was verified after download.
4. **Analyses defined after results were seen.** Sections 7 and 8 of the addendum (near-stationary plan rate, stop episodes, blocked time) were written after parts of the gate-off run had been looked at. Each section states what had been seen when it was written. They are reported as post hoc and exploratory.
5. **The gate-off controller is a copy, not a setting.** `alpamayo_bridge_agent_nogate.py` differs from the hashed controller in two places: the gate condition no longer forces the target speed to zero or applies the holding brake, and six extra fields are written to the step log (extent, whether the gate would have fired, target speed, raw throttle, raw brake, controller variant). The hashed file itself is unchanged (SHA-256 `082c6ecc…10d3`).
